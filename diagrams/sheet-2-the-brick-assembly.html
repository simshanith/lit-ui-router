THE BRICK ASSEMBLY

ALTITUDE 2 — one baseplate, four bricks, 25 authored files · REV B: the companions redrawn as an exploded LEGO assembly, every coupling named to its API call, source counted 2026-08-16

GENERAL NOTES

Why bricks. Rev A drew these packages as sockets and panels and conceded the form broke. The mechanism they actually share is a standardised coupling: each companion attaches to @uirouter/core through a published extension point, none of them attaches to another, and any one can be left in the box without disturbing the rest. That is a stud, and a stud is worth drawing. So this is an exploded isometric — the LEGO instruction manual's own idiom — with a numbered part per package, a drop line onto the exact stud it takes, and a parts callout. Nothing is drawn seated, because a seated assembly hides the undersides, and the undersides are the argument.

The plate is core, not this package. Sheet 4's finding decides it: every limb in the family declares @uirouter/core as a peer and touches nothing else. lit-ui-router is therefore brick 1, not the ground — and the drawing is honest about the one place the metaphor strains: class UIRouterLit extends UIRouter is moulded onto the plate, not snapped to it. What the drawing then shows is that the graft is thin anyway. Everything Lit-specific arrives through two published seams — this.plugin(servicesPlugin) and this.stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder) — plus urlService.listen()/sync() to start the thing. Three calls. That is the whole renderer coupling.

One stud is a seat, not a socket. router.plugin() is the back rail: a single method that anything may queue on, which is why the spare-parts box is drawn at all — @uirouter/visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx all register through it and none of them knows this repo exists. One seat on that rail is ringed red, because it behaves differently: a router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin is a swap for core's pushStateLocation, never an addition. That package peers @uirouter/core and nothing else — it has never heard of Lit, and would work identically under the React or Angular adapters.

Brick 3 is the only brick-to-brick coupling in the set, and it is a small one. lit-ui-router-mobx seats on brick 1 through UIRouterLitElement.seekRouter(host) — a bubbling ui-router-context event, published precisely as the dependency-injection primitive for external reactivity systems — and then reaches the plate directly: one transitionService.onSuccess({}, update) hook, memoised one-per-router by RouterStore.for(), whose update() reads globals.current, globals.params and the last successful transition into MobX observables. It observes; it never writes router state. 133 lines, one stud on the brick above and two on the plate below.

The fourth brick has no stud here at all, and that is the design. ui-router-server declares @uirouter/core as an optional peer (peerDependenciesMeta); its default 'matcher' tier is dependency-free pattern matching and never loads core, and its 'simulate' tier reaches a plate of its own behind a lazy import() — new UIRouter() with servicesPlugin and memoryLocationPlugin, built fresh per resolution because core mutates registrations. Drawing it over a dashed second plate is the only truthful placement: it is the same mould, a different assembly, and the tie back to the client plate is crossed out.

Massing, quantized. Continuous mass would have made these bricks unbuildable shapes, so the census is rounded to LEGO: plan is one stud per 150 sloc rounded up to the next standard shape (1×1, 1×2, 2×2, 2×3, 2×4), height is one course per three authored files. The result is legible and it is a finding — the two bricks that carry a renderer and a server are 2×4s; the two that plug the router into something are 1×1 tiles, 105 and 133 lines. A companion that needed to be a 2×4 would be lit-ui-router's problem to absorb, not a package. The baseplate is deliberately not massed: 80 files and 5,272 lines of core is ground, and ground has no height.
