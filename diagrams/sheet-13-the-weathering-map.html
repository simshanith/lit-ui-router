GENERAL NOTES

Method — one pass, renames followed. Every file in sheet 7's universe was dated from a single git log -M --name-status pass over all 483 commits, walked newest to oldest with rename chains followed backwards — equivalent to per-file --follow (spot-verified against it, including tools/shared/workspace.ts, which the plain path log mis-dates by a week) but one process instead of 264. Per file: first-commit date, last-touch date, touch count. Merge commits list no files under the default log, so a touch is a non-merge commit — the standard convention. Footprints and placements are sheet 7 rev B's, reused as pure geometry so the two sheets reconcile by eye; the weathering itself is dated 2026-08-17 at HEAD, where the city has 264 files to sheet 7's 258 — members 1, 2 and 9 grew spec files in the intervening day, and the schedule carries the current counts.

The bands are campaigns, not round numbers. The age histogram is not a slope, it is three spikes: files born around day 392 (July 2025), around day 210–228 (January 2026), and within the last 44 days — with nothing between them, because the repo has source births or touches in only four of its fourteen calendar months (commits in five: 42 · 103 · 3 · 224 · 111). So the map bins by season, where the distribution actually cuts: SEASON I, the port — 26 files that arrived 2025-07-21 with the upstream sample-app lineage; SEASON II, the winter works — 18 files including the typedoc plugin and the examples; SEASON III, the summer — 220 files, 83% of the city, none older than 2026-07-04. Churn tiers come from the per-block distribution the same way: touches-per-file has a clean gap between 4.6 and 6.0, so HOT is ≥6; the per-file median is 2, so COLD is below it. SEALED is idle >180 days — the idle histogram is empty from 60 to 180, and exactly six files sit beyond the gap.

The verdict: age and churn run opposite ways. Mean touches per file falls monotonically with youth — ×9.5 for the port cohort, ×5.5 for winter, ×2.3 for summer — and 104 of 264 files (39%) have been touched exactly once, ever. The port's masonry is not museum stone: 14 of its 26 files were touched in the last month, ui-sref.ts is at ×15 with the last chisel-mark dated 2026-08-16, and the two most-weathered walls in the city are the port-era docs/.vitepress pair — config.ts at ×24 and vite.config.ts at ×19. What survives from the first day is precisely what keeps being worked.

What each district's weather says. The instrument yard — 4,684 sloc, the city's largest district by mass — logged zero touches before 2026 and is almost entirely summer stone at ×1.9 per file: infrastructure arrived late, fast, and mostly settled on the first cut. Its one winter building is also the map's sealed wing: the typedoc plugin's symbols/ trio, untouched 220 days. The shopfront splits in two: docs runs the hottest block-average in the city (×7.1/f src) while examples holds the oldest untouched stone — two vite configs idle 228 days. And the smallest building on the map has the sharpest weather: navigation-location-plugin's single January wall has been chiselled ten times, the highest per-file churn anywhere — matching its history as the extracted plugin that every routing change touches.

Approximations, so the numbers stay honest. Dates are author dates (%as); on this repo's squash-merge convention they equal the merge day of the PR that landed the change. Rename detection is git's -M heuristic: a file moved and rewritten in one commit can read as a fresh birth — the July 2026 scripts/ → tools/ graduations are dated to that graduation where git saw no rename, which the yard's notes above already state as its story. Generated and vendored trees were never in the universe (sheet 7's exclusions); nothing else was excluded.
