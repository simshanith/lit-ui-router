GENERAL NOTES

Method — the sheet surveys its own drawer. Every station, drawer, tab and edge on this plate is introspected at build time by diagrams/generator/census-atlas.mjs: a probe is any census-*.mjs that writes a plate; a shared instrument is one the probes import (there are two — basis.mjs and census-query.mjs); a tier is decided by which basis primitive the source actually calls — materialize alone is T1, historyLog is T2, installDeps is T3; and a reader is any generator file that constructs the plate's URL. The introspection throws rather than draws if a plate has anything but exactly one writer, if a probe names a plate that is not filed, if a drawing reads a plate nothing writes, or — the claim this sheet makes loudest — if the 15 filed plates disagree about the ref they were measured at. They do not: all 15 are pinned to origin/main @ eb32b4e · commit 2026-09-03. What is editorial here is what is editorial on every sheet: placement, the short basis labels, and this prose.

The hero is the fan-out. One scc --by-file pass over the archive produces census-files.json, 581 rows of per-file measurement, and eleven things read it: ten stations that are group-by queries over the same rows — census-bricks.mjs, census-city.mjs, census-doors.mjs, census-handoff.mjs, census-npm.mjs, census-shadow.mjs, census-steam.mjs, census-weather.mjs, census-yard.mjs, census-overview.mjs — plus the gallery cover. That is the architecture's whole argument in one edge bundle: the city census, the yard census, the brick census, the weather map and the steam channel cannot disagree about how many files there are, because there is only one count. Cross-sheet totals reconcile by construction, not by discipline.

Three tiers, and what each one costs. six stations are T1 — the archive measured once, or a query over that measurement. two are T2: one git log over the same ref, with the window and «today» derived from the ref's own commit date. seven are T3, and they are the expensive half of the office: the archive is not just extracted but installed — corepack pnpm install --frozen-lockfile, then the tree's own node_modules/.bin/turbo invoked directly — so that turbo dry-runs, node_modules closures, vite and rolldown builds all measure the ref rather than a checkout. The harness bar above the T3 band is the only sub-instrument drawn, because it is the only one that changes what a measurement means.

Five faults of the old regime, and the answer to each. Before this pipeline: (1) the basis was whatever was checked out, with hand-rolled skip lists per probe — answered by one git archive per ref, the tracked set exactly as git defines it; (2) member lists were frozen 30-entry arrays that predated a package's graduation, with existsSync guards that let a missing directory count as zero — answered by discovering members from the archive's own workspace file; (3) numbers travelled by clipboard, printed by a probe and pasted into a sheet — answered by filed plates and lookups that throw on a missing row; (4) time was hard-coded, so re-running today still aged files against last August — answered by deriving every date from the measured commit; (5) inputs came from out of band — a dead scratch clone, vanished tmp/ generators, npm dates typed by hand — answered by seven scripted execution probes and a registry probe. The one relic still in the drawer is drawn struck through: census.mjs, a working-tree walker imported by nothing and writing no plate.

What the drawing does not show, and the schedule does. With 15 drawers and 16 reading sheets, a complete edge render would be unreadable, so the drawing keeps the shape — one horizontal writes edge per station, a bundled accent bus for the master plate, thin curves for the rest — and the plate schedule carries the facts: writer, tier, basis and every reader, for all 15 plates. Two counts are worth reading off it. Zero plates are unread: every filed measurement is on a drawing somewhere, which was not true of this pipeline a week ago. And three of the atlas's 19 sheets still have no plate behind them — sheet 5, sheet 6, sheet 14 — because they draw arguments, mechanisms and metering that no census produces. This sheet is honestly among them: it has no plate of its own; it reads the generator.

Why a flow graph and not a city. Sheet 3 refuses to draw a task manager as a place, and the same refusal applies here. A pipeline's truth is dataflow — ref to archive to measurement to plate to drawing — and the lego/containment vocabulary the atlas uses elsewhere would encode a claim about what contains what, which is exactly the wrong claim about a pipeline. The vocabulary that fits is the one the atlas already owns: probes as survey instruments, snapshots as filed plates, sheets as finished drawings. Tier is station type; the cabinet is a cabinet; the rack is a rack. The node and edge arrays behind this drawing (62 nodes over four kinds, 82 edges over writes, reads and imports) are also the data model the interactive lane will mount — the picture is a query, not a hand-drawing.
