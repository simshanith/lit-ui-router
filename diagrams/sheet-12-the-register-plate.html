GENERAL NOTES

Method. turbo run ci --dry=json and turbo run ci:main --dry=json against a materialized, installed archive of the ref — surveyed at origin/main @ 65e2843 (2026-09-11) · turbo 2.10.12 — and the same for build, lint and pack:all in the schedule. Every number on the plate is read from diagrams/data/census-plate.json, the checked-in snapshot census-plate.mjs writes; this sheet holds placement and prose only. A node is one (package, task) pair turbo placed in the graph. A node is command-bearing when its resolved command is not the literal <NONEXISTENT> — turbo's marker for a task it had to invent because something depends on it in that package. An edge is real only when both of its ends are. No duration is drawn anywhere: wall-clock comparisons here are confounded by cache state and by the task counts themselves, so this plate measures shape and nothing else.

Why an inventory and not a city. Sheet 3 argues that a task manager is not a place; drawing this graph in isometric would smuggle back the geography that sheet's note denies. What a CI graph actually is, is a register: a fixed set of names crossed against a fixed set of packages, with most of the intersections empty. The honest form is the plate that shape already implies — a punchcard, read by column.

The finding. 660 nodes; 195 run a command. 1,544 dependency edges; 73 join two real tasks. Of the 19 fanned names, 5 carry a ^self chain — build, build:types, test, test:coverage, transit — and a self-chain is what mints placeholders: turbo needs a node in every package to hang the chain on, whether or not that package has such a script. 3 names are 100% placeholder in all 35 packages: transit (which has no implementation anywhere in the repo — it exists purely as an edge), and the two roll-ups ci:pull_request and ci. The plate's rightmost columns and its leftmost are, in the strictest sense, empty.

Depth is mostly scaffolding too. The deepest chain is 13 nodes and 4 of them run anything. The longest chain of consecutive real nodes is 6 — and it is 6 test tasks in a row, serialized by ^test and nothing else: no task in that chain consumes an artifact from the one above it. That is the price of a self-chain used for ordering rather than for data flow, and it is what a plate makes visible that a node-and-arrow render never does.

What the plate is not evidence for. Placeholders are cheap — turbo schedules and skips them, and the phantom share is not a runtime cost. The argument is about legibility: 70% of what a maintainer sees in --graph output is scaffolding, and the two ratios worth watching are the real-node share per column and the real-edge count. Note also the one tier that never appears here at all: the repo's 13 cache:false definitions — four writers, six persistent servers, two live-network readers and one host-bound measurement (docs#check:embeds, #703) — are all outside every ci:* graph by design. A cache hit on any of them would be wrong, and the gate depends on their read-only twins instead (format:check for format).

The overlay. ci:main is not a different pipeline; it swallows ci:pull_request whole and adds 37 nodes across 4 names — ci:main, test:engines, test:matrix, check:pack — of which 5 are command-bearing. The main-branch graph is 6% larger and buys three engine tests, one d.ts back-test and one pack check.

Where the real edges are, and are not. The docs:api column is punched in every package it stands in: docs#build names its four producers directly rather than walking ^docs:api through devDependencies it never imports (#693). That is this plate's thesis arriving from the other side — scaffolding leaves the graph and the real work stays. It is also why real→real edges are a small fraction of 1,544: most edges land on a node that runs nothing.
