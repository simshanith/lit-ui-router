GENERAL NOTES

Method. turbo run ci --dry=json and turbo run ci:main --dry=json against a materialized, installed archive of the ref — surveyed at origin/main @ 35c6766 (2026-09-03) · turbo 2.10.11 — and the same for build, lint and pack:all in the schedule. Every number on the plate is read from diagrams/data/census-plate.json, the checked-in snapshot census-plate.mjs writes; this sheet holds placement and prose only. A node is one (package, task) pair turbo placed in the graph. A node is command-bearing when its resolved command is not the literal <NONEXISTENT> — turbo's marker for a task it had to invent because something depends on it in that package. An edge is real only when both of its ends are. No duration is drawn anywhere: wall-clock comparisons here are confounded by cache state and by the task counts themselves, so this plate measures shape and nothing else.

Why an inventory and not a city. Sheet 3 argues that a task manager is not a place; drawing this graph in isometric would smuggle back the geography that sheet's note denies. What a CI graph actually is, is a register: a fixed set of names crossed against a fixed set of packages, with most of the intersections empty. The honest form is the plate that shape already implies — a punchcard, read by column.

The finding. 590 nodes; 176 run a command. 1,504 dependency edges; 126 join two real tasks. Of the 19 fanned names, 5 carry a ^self chain — build, build:types, test, test:coverage, transit — and a self-chain is what mints placeholders: turbo needs a node in every package to hang the chain on, whether or not that package has such a script. 3 names are 100% placeholder in all 31 packages: transit (which has no implementation anywhere in the repo — it exists purely as an edge), and the two roll-ups ci:pull_request and ci. The plate's rightmost columns and its leftmost are, in the strictest sense, empty.

Depth is mostly scaffolding too. The deepest chain is 13 nodes and 5 of them run anything. The longest chain of consecutive real nodes is 7 — and it is 7 test tasks in a row, serialized by ^test and nothing else: no task in that chain consumes an artifact from the one above it. That is the price of a self-chain used for ordering rather than for data flow, and it is what a plate makes visible that a node-and-arrow render never does.

What the plate is not evidence for. Placeholders are cheap — turbo schedules and skips them, and the phantom share is not a runtime cost. The argument is about legibility: 70% of what a maintainer sees in --graph output is scaffolding, and the two ratios worth watching are the real-node share per column and the real-edge count. Note also the one tier that never appears here at all: the repo's 12 cache:false definitions — four writers, six persistent servers and two live-network readers — are all outside every ci:* graph by design. A cache hit on any of them would be wrong, and the gate depends on their read-only twins instead (format:check for format).

The overlay. ci:main is not a different pipeline; it swallows ci:pull_request whole and adds 33 nodes across 4 names — ci:main, test:engines, test:matrix, check:pack — of which 5 are command-bearing. The main-branch graph is 6% larger and buys three engine tests, one d.ts back-test and one pack check.

Rev B — census refresh, 2026-08-31. Two weeks and one release (lit-ui-router@1.10.0) after the first printing, the plate was re-punched from a fresh --dry=json. Three rows joined the tools block — @tools/eslint-ts-parser, @tools/lint-elements and @tools/warn-lanes (#639) — taking the register from 27 packages to 30 and the graph from 483 nodes / 154 real to 535 / 165; edges 1,280 → 1,375, real edges 116 → 117. The shape is what held: the phantom share moved only 68% → 69%, the eighteen fanned names are the same eighteen, and all three new rows punch the same sparse pattern every small instrument does: typecheck, lint, format:check — three holes of eighteen — plus test for @tools/warn-lanes, which is the only one of the three with a suite. That is the plate's own thesis holding under a new measurement: a new member adds eighteen stations to the register and punches three or four of them. Elsewhere: the ragged tail gained //#lint:elements (25 → 26 singletons, fifteen of them root); the deepest chain grew a rung to thirteen — still five real — because @tools/warn-lanes#build:types now sits above build_and_test; the longest all-real chain shortened from seven test tasks to six; and the uncacheable tier was recounted across all seventeen turbo.json files rather than the root alone, which is twelve definitions, not seven. Still none of them reachable from ci.

Rev C — off the plate. The hand-pasted constants are gone: rows, columns, cells, tallies, the overlay and the schedule are all read from diagrams/data/census-plate.json at draw time, and the sheet throws rather than draws if a pipeline or a fanned name it needs is missing. Re-surveyed at origin/main @ 35c6766, the graph has grown again: packages/eslint-plugin-lit-ui-router is the fifth publishable package, taking the register from 30 fanned rows to 31 and the graph from 535 nodes / 165 real to 590 / 176; edges 1,375 → 1,504, real edges 117 → 126, phantom share 69% → 70%. There is also a 19th fanned column — check:dev-split, the dev-warning split guard, command-bearing in 1 package and a placeholder in the other 30 — which is the same story the eighteen told, one column wider. The ragged tail took the new package's three singletons (lint:docs, lint:rules, test:oxlint) and stands at 29; the deepest chain is still 13 rungs with 5 real, but the longest all-real chain went back up to 7 test tasks — a new publishable package with a suite is exactly the sort of member that lengthens it.
