THE RENDER LOOP

ALTITUDE 1 — lit-ui-router 1.9.0 · the client circuit · REV D: the loop routed on the iso grid

GENERAL NOTES

Why redraw a 2D loop in 3D: the cycle itself is flat, but the third axis is free to carry the relations flat arrows kept fumbling. Containment: the child <ui-view> literally stands on its parent's roof — nesting is stacking, not a box-in-a-box. Propagation: uiSrefTarget events rise past the document's plates to the uiSrefActive perch, which is how link tracking works — no registry, just bubbling. Instrumentation: TransitionController is a skybridge that taps the hall without standing on the route.

The document breaks the metaphor on purpose. Every other station is a building; the document is drawn the way Firefox's old Tilt inspector drew it — a browser window whose DOM rises as stacked plates by depth, window → body → app shell → <ui-view> content → the <a> element on top. It is the one structure on this sheet that really is layered, so it alone earns the literal treatment.

Service discovery is still DOM events. A nested view finds its parent with a composed context CustomEvent; every uiSref announces its targetState upward. The document tree is the dependency graph — which is exactly why the drawing can stand everything in one window.

The five doors are one seam. The bare entry registers custom elements as a side effect; ./pure is the same API with registration torn off. sideEffects in the manifest names exactly the four register files.

THE BRICK ASSEMBLY

ALTITUDE 2 — one baseplate, four bricks, 25 authored files · REV B: the companions redrawn as an exploded LEGO assembly, every coupling named to its API call, source counted 2026-08-16

GENERAL NOTES

Why bricks. Rev A drew these packages as sockets and panels and conceded the form broke. The mechanism they actually share is a standardised coupling: each companion attaches to @uirouter/core through a published extension point, none of them attaches to another, and any one can be left in the box without disturbing the rest. That is a stud, and a stud is worth drawing. So this is an exploded isometric — the LEGO instruction manual's own idiom — with a numbered part per package, a drop line onto the exact stud it takes, and a parts callout. Nothing is drawn seated, because a seated assembly hides the undersides, and the undersides are the argument.

The plate is core, not this package. Sheet 4's finding decides it: every limb in the family declares @uirouter/core as a peer and touches nothing else. lit-ui-router is therefore brick 1, not the ground — and the drawing is honest about the one place the metaphor strains: class UIRouterLit extends UIRouter is moulded onto the plate, not snapped to it. What the drawing then shows is that the graft is thin anyway. Everything Lit-specific arrives through two published seams — this.plugin(servicesPlugin) and this.stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder) — plus urlService.listen()/sync() to start the thing. Three calls. That is the whole renderer coupling.

One stud is a seat, not a socket. router.plugin() is the back rail: a single method that anything may queue on, which is why the spare-parts box is drawn at all — @uirouter/visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx all register through it and none of them knows this repo exists. One seat on that rail is ringed red, because it behaves differently: a router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin is a swap for core's pushStateLocation, never an addition. That package peers @uirouter/core and nothing else — it has never heard of Lit, and would work identically under the React or Angular adapters.

Brick 3 is the only brick-to-brick coupling in the set, and it is a small one. lit-ui-router-mobx seats on brick 1 through UIRouterLitElement.seekRouter(host) — a bubbling ui-router-context event, published precisely as the dependency-injection primitive for external reactivity systems — and then reaches the plate directly: one transitionService.onSuccess({}, update) hook, memoised one-per-router by RouterStore.for(), whose update() reads globals.current, globals.params and the last successful transition into MobX observables. It observes; it never writes router state. 133 lines, one stud on the brick above and two on the plate below.

The fourth brick has no stud here at all, and that is the design. ui-router-server declares @uirouter/core as an optional peer (peerDependenciesMeta); its default 'matcher' tier is dependency-free pattern matching and never loads core, and its 'simulate' tier reaches a plate of its own behind a lazy import() — new UIRouter() with servicesPlugin and memoryLocationPlugin, built fresh per resolution because core mutates registrations. Drawing it over a dashed second plate is the only truthful placement: it is the same mould, a different assembly, and the tie back to the client plate is crossed out.

Massing, quantized. Continuous mass would have made these bricks unbuildable shapes, so the census is rounded to LEGO: plan is one stud per 150 sloc rounded up to the next standard shape (1×1, 1×2, 2×2, 2×3, 2×4), height is one course per three authored files. The result is legible and it is a finding — the two bricks that carry a renderer and a server are 2×4s; the two that plug the router into something are 1×1 tiles, 105 and 133 lines. A companion that needed to be a 2×4 would be lit-ui-router's problem to absorb, not a package. The baseplate is deliberately not massed: 80 files and 5,272 lines of core is ground, and ground has no height.

GENERAL NOTES

This plate is the legibility companion to sheet 2. The exploded assembly (sheet 2, THE BRICK ASSEMBLY) shows the whole stack and where every brick falls; this plate isolates the couplings and draws each one at reading size. It deliberately returns to rev A’s spatial arrangement — core central, companions at its right, the server in a request lane below the no-DOM line — but renders the packages as shallow solids and spends the recovered space entirely on the joints.

Every joint is drawn disengaged. A seated plug hides both mating faces, so nothing here is seated: each stud on the wall stops 18px short of its plug’s mouth, the dashed centreline is the engagement, and the call that makes the coupling — stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder), urlService.listen()/.sync(), router.plugin(servicesPlugin), transitionService.onSuccess({}, update), globals.current/.params — is lettered on the connection itself, not in a schedule at the edge of the sheet.

One connection point is a seat, not a stud, and it is drawn as a keyed recess. A router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin does not add to the wall — it swaps: its keyed red plug approaches the LOCATION SEAT while a dashed ghost plug, core’s own pushStateLocation, leaves it. The rail above behaves the opposite way: router.plugin() is one method with any number of seats, which is where visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx would queue (sheet 4).

The one brick-to-brick coupling gets its own axis. lit-ui-router-mobx is the only companion that touches another companion: its upward socket waits under a stud on lit-ui-router’s underside — seekRouter(host), the bubbling ui-router-context event — and everything else it does is read-only against the wall: one memoised onSuccess hook on stud C, observations of globals on stud E, never a write.

THE ROUTING STRATA

ALTITUDE 6 — routing in general · the altitude where prose outranks pictures

GENERAL NOTES

The definition that survives every stratum: a router is an address space, a matching function, and transition semantics. BGP matches prefixes and forwards with no memory; an HTTP framework matches path patterns into a stateless response; an edge ruleset rewrites and proxies; a SPA router matches a URL into a view tree through a cancellable lifecycle; a state router matches into a named hierarchy through guarded, resolving transitions.

What changes with ascent is the transition. At the bedrock a transition is a forwarding decision that forgets itself immediately. Each stratum upward, transitions acquire more: statefulness, interceptability, cancellation, resolved data, meaning. By the surface a transition is a first-class object with a lifecycle — which is precisely the layer sheet 1 drew.

Why this sheet is mostly writing: no mechanism spans these strata — an IP hop and a ui-view activation share a definition, not a wire. Drawing more than a core sample here would be decoration, and the reference's own rule applies: the drawing earns exactly as much ink as the mechanism deserves.

THE REGISTER PLATE

ALTITUDE 3¼ — the monorepo as its CI reads it · every task node punched · turbo 2.10.11 · REV B: census refresh 2026-08-31 — three new members, three new rows, the phantom share held

GENERAL NOTES

Method. turbo run ci --dry=json and turbo run ci:main --dry=json from the repository worktree, turbo 2.10.11, recounted 2026-08-31; the same for build, lint and pack:all in the schedule. A node is one (package, task) pair turbo placed in the graph. A node is command-bearing when its resolved command is not the literal <NONEXISTENT> — turbo's marker for a task it had to invent because something depends on it in that package. An edge is real only when both of its ends are. No duration is drawn anywhere: wall-clock comparisons here are confounded by cache state and by the task counts themselves, so this plate measures shape and nothing else.

Why an inventory and not a city. Sheet 3 argues that a task manager is not a place; drawing this graph in isometric would smuggle back the geography that sheet's note denies. What a CI graph actually is, is a register: a fixed set of names crossed against a fixed set of packages, with most of the intersections empty. The honest form is the plate that shape already implies — a punchcard, read by column.

The finding. 535 nodes; 165 run a command. 1,375 dependency edges; 117 join two real tasks. Of the eighteen fanned names, five carry a ^self chain — build, build:types, test, test:coverage, transit — and a self-chain is what mints placeholders: turbo needs a node in every package to hang the chain on, whether or not that package has such a script. Three names are 100% placeholder in all 30 packages: transit (which has no implementation anywhere in the repo — it exists purely as an edge), and the two roll-ups ci:pull_request and ci. The plate's rightmost columns and its leftmost are, in the strictest sense, empty.

Depth is mostly scaffolding too. The deepest chain is thirteen nodes and five of them run anything. The longest chain of consecutive real nodes is six — and it is six test tasks in a row, serialized by ^test and nothing else: no task in that chain consumes an artifact from the one above it. That is the price of a self-chain used for ordering rather than for data flow, and it is what a plate makes visible that a node-and-arrow render never does.

What the plate is not evidence for. Placeholders are cheap — turbo schedules and skips them, and the phantom share is not a runtime cost. The argument is about legibility: 69% of what a maintainer sees in --graph output is scaffolding, and the two ratios worth watching are the real-node share per column and the real-edge count. Note also the one tier that never appears here at all: the repo's twelve cache:false definitions — four writers, six persistent servers and two live-network readers — are all outside every ci:* graph by design. A cache hit on any of them would be wrong, and the gate depends on their read-only twins instead (format:check for format).

The overlay. ci:main is not a different pipeline; it swallows ci:pull_request whole and adds 32 nodes across four names — ci:main, test:engines, test:matrix, check:pack — of which five are command-bearing. The main-branch graph is 6% larger and buys three engine tests, one d.ts back-test and one pack check.

The shape is what held: the phantom share moved only 68% → 69%, the eighteen fanned names are the same eighteen, and all three new rows punch the same sparse pattern every small instrument does: typecheck, lint, format:check — three holes of eighteen — plus test for @tools/warn-lanes, which is the only one of the three with a suite.
