GENERAL NOTES

This plate is sheet 2A with the joints made live. Sheet 2 explodes the assembly and sheet 2A draws each coupling at reading size; both letter the API call that makes the joint. This one drops the API entirely and draws the other contract — the one npm install reads. Its arrangement is deliberately 2A’s: @uirouter/core as the socket wall at the left with lit above it, the companions in a column at its right in 2A’s order, and the server below them with the coupling that exists only to be crossed out.

An edge is one declaration, not a relationship. Every line is a single entry in a published package.json — its section (peerDependencies or dependencies), its declared range, and whether peerDependenciesMeta marks it optional. devDependencies are not drawn: they bind this workspace and never reach a consumer’s install. Of 12 contracts the five published packages declare, 7 land on a node that is on this bench; the other 5 are filed on the plate and read out in the panel under OFF THE BENCH.

The ranges are the ranges that ship, not the ones written in the file. Every spec in this repo is a catalog reference — catalog:publishedPeer, not ^6.0.8 — which pnpm rewrites at pack time. census-couplings.mjs resolves each one against the archive’s own pnpm-workspace.yaml and the plate carries both, so the panel can show you what is written and what a consumer would see. @uirouter/core and lit carry no range of their own on this bench: their versions (6.1.2 and 2.8.0 · 3.3.3) come from the lockfile, which is the only honest source for a resolution.

The whole deps-to-peers decision is legible in one column. 10 of the 12 contracts are peers and only 2 are dependencies — and neither of the two shipped dependencies is a router. lit-ui-router declares @uirouter/core at ^6.0.8 and lit at ^2.0.0 || ^3.0.0 as peers, and keeps exactly one thing in its tarball’s dependency list: @oxc-project/runtime at >=0.50.0, a runtime helper deliberately declared as wide as it can honestly be. lit-ui-router-mobx goes further and declares no dependency at all: all 4 of its contracts are peers, including the one brick-to-brick joint on the bench — lit-ui-router ^1.7.0, a floor the flagship has since left behind at 1.11.2 without breaking it.

The lit range is a lane, and it is drawn as one. ^2.0.0 || ^3.0.0 is not a loose bound; it is a supported compatibility lane, and the same range appears on both packages that touch lit. The bench shows the consequence: the lockfile resolves two lit majors (2.8.0 · 3.3.3), because the compat lane is tested, not merely permitted.

Two contracts are drawn in red, and they are the interesting ones. ui-router-server’s @uirouter/core is an optional peer at ^6.0.8 — sheet 2A draws that same tie crossed out, because the default matcher tier never loads the wall. And eslint-plugin-lit-ui-router stands in a bay of its own: it declares 2 contracts, eslint ^9.0.0 || ^10.0.0 and a shipped eslint-plugin-lit, and not one of them touches anything else on this bench. It is a published package of this repo that couples to none of its siblings — which is exactly why it is drawn here rather than left out.

Counting rules. 4 of the five published packages name @uirouter/core; 1 of those name it optionally. ui-router-navigation-location-plugin declares exactly one contract in the world — @uirouter/core ^6.0.8 — which is the plate’s own way of saying it has never heard of Lit. Massing and storeys are the brick schedule’s: front-face area tracks sloc, the 1×1 companions are clamped up to a legible minimum, and a building’s storeys are its courses band from census-bricks.json, never a pipeline tier.
