GENERAL NOTES

One box, several models. Sheet 2 draws every family brick in one assembly on @uirouter/core. The repository's own consumers — 18 consumers: the sample apps, the docs site and the examples at the cabinet's sha — pin seven distinct sets of family parts, each manifest closed over its family peers, and this plate draws each set as the model it makes: its parts at sheet 2's mould, standing on the runtime plates those parts peer. The eighth model, build VIII, is the atlas's own app: its manifest at www/atlas.lit-ui-router.dev/app, read from git archive www/atlas @ 7ba404cb, off the cabinet's tree, and drawn accent-edged on its own branch the way sheet A4 draws the atlas deploy. Every count on this sheet reads both ways, the cabinet's tree first and “with the atlas app” after. Build I is the most built, lit-ui-router alone on @uirouter/core and lit, by 5 consumers. One consumer pins no family part at all (sample-app-lit-e2e), and is a consumer rather than a build.

No model mixes two reactivity runtimes. effect, mobx and signal-polyfill are each reached by a family binding — lit-ui-router-mobx, lit-ui-router-effect, lit-ui-router-signals, ui-router-effect and ui-router-signals — and no consumer in either reading mixes two of them; the build stops if one does. The bindings are alternates in the box, the way one set's instructions show a car or a plane, not both.

Ground and stacking follow the dependency. A family brick peers lit or effect, not the reverse, and no runtime on this plate peers another — both read from each row's own peerDependencies — so each runtime is a plate, the parts stand on them, and the plates lie on plain ground. @uirouter/core is a plate like the others: nine of the ten family bricks peer it, and it is the baseplate of every model, laid first at the left, the way sheet 2 seats the family on it. A part stands on the part it peers when that part is in the same build, as it does on sheet 2; otherwise it seats on its binding, its one runtime peer in the build other than @uirouter/core and lit, and on core when none remains (lit-ui-router, ui-router-navigation-location-plugin and ui-router-server); lit is a plate beside the model, its stud ringed under no brick. A build stands on the runtimes its consumers declare and on every required peer its parts carry, which the installer adds whether or not a manifest names it; an optional peer no consumer declares stays off the model.

One scale. The family keeps sheet 2's mould exactly — 1 stud per 150 sloc rounded up the brick shapes, a course per 3 authored files. A runtime is baseplates at sheet 2's baseplate footprint, 8×6 = 48 studs, one stud per 150 sloc of one shipped build — the files reachable from its exports entries, the development condition where one is shipped (@lit-labs/ssr-client) — laid a plate at a time and stacked when it overflows, the remainder on top at its own size, three plates to a brick course: effect is 16 plates · 15 of 8×6 + a 4×5, eslint is 9 plates · 8 of 8×6 + a 6×6 and hono is 2 plates · 1 of 8×6 + a 4×4. Under 48 studs a runtime is one plate at its own size, the nearest rectangle: @lit-labs/ssr 3×3, @lit-labs/ssr-client 1×2, @uirouter/core 6×6, lit 1×1, mobx 6×6 and signal-polyfill 2×2. A package's dependencies are a number in the schedule rather than area on the plate: @lit-labs/ssr builds 1,083 lines over a closure of 28,345, @lit-labs/ssr-client builds 282 lines over a closure of 4,564 and lit builds 39 lines over a closure of 4,282. Every model on lit stands on a 1×1: six of the seven builds, seven of eight with the atlas app, and the runtime they run on is in its closure.

What stays in the box. lit-ui-router-ssr is carried by no consumer at the cabinet's tree, so no model of that reading draws it; with the atlas app, lit-ui-router-ssr stands in build VIII. @lit-labs/ssr, @lit-labs/ssr-client and hono are plates no model stands on at the cabinet's tree — @lit-labs/ssr-client and hono optional and pinned by no consumer there, @lit-labs/ssr peered only by a part no consumer there carries; with the atlas app, build VIII stands on @lit-labs/ssr and @lit-labs/ssr-client, and hono stays spare. They stand in the dashed register under the box, each line reading the cabinet's tree first and the atlas app after, and the spare plates are drawn there rather than on the ground. Build II's manifests differ in what they pin and what they take as a peer; the schedule says which.
