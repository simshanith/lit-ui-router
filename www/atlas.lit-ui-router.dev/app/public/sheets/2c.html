GENERAL NOTES

One box, several models. Sheet 2 draws every family brick in one assembly on @uirouter/core. The repository's own consumers — 20 consumers: the sample apps, the docs site and the examples at the cabinet's sha — pin ten distinct sets of family parts, each manifest closed over its family peers, and this plate draws each set as the model it makes: its parts at sheet 2's mould, standing on the runtime plates those parts peer. The eleventh model, build XI, is the atlas's own app: its manifest at www/atlas.lit-ui-router.dev/app, read from git archive www/atlas @ 08a98096, off the cabinet's tree, and drawn accent-edged on its own branch the way sheet A4 draws the atlas deploy. Every count on this sheet reads both ways, the cabinet's tree first and “with the atlas app” after. Build I is the most built, lit-ui-router alone on @uirouter/core and lit, by 6 consumers. One consumer pins no family part at all (sample-app-lit-e2e), and is a consumer rather than a build.

No model mixes two reactivity runtimes. effect, mobx and signal-polyfill are each reached by a family binding — lit-ui-router-mobx, lit-ui-router-effect, lit-ui-router-signals, ui-router-effect, ui-router-signals and ui-router-mobx — and no consumer in either reading mixes two of them; the build stops if one does. The bindings are alternates in the box, the way one set's instructions show a car or a plane, not both.

Ground and stacking follow the dependency. A family brick peers lit or effect, not the reverse, and no runtime on this plate peers another — both read from each row's own peerDependencies — so each runtime is a plate, the parts stand on them, and the plates lie on plain ground. @uirouter/core is a plate like the others: eleven of the twelve family bricks peer it, and it is the baseplate of every model, laid first at the left, the way sheet 2 seats the family on it. A part stands on the part it peers when that part is in the same build, as it does on sheet 2, and the rest seat on core at sheet 2's places measured from core's right edge, packed from the back where sheet 2 seats them off its client plate; a part that peers no core seats on its one runtime (eslint-plugin-lit-ui-router). Every other runtime a brick peers abuts core and the brick reaches it, unless the brick it stands on already carries it: a brick on core straddles the seam onto a flat runtime plate one stud deep, the way lit-ui-router stands on both core and lit, and a brick that cannot straddle, one on another brick or one beside a stack, reaches it by a cord: a string with end studs, two 1×1 round plates joined by a cord, one seated on a ringed stud of the brick's top and the other on a ringed stud of the plate, the cord leaving each plate's own stud, from the nearest pair of free studs whose cord clears every brick and stack. A brick standing on another brick hangs its cord instead from a free stud of its underside, the round plate's stud seated in the brick as on a stud, wherever that cord drops clear of every other brick and stack — brick 5 in build III, brick 6 in build VI and brick 6 in build XI. Nothing abuts core from behind: a stack taller than core stands at its left or right side, and a flat plate along its front, at the right beside the seam or at the left, wherever every cord to it runs clear of every brick and stack; each plate rings one stud per brick coupled to it. The atlas app's model spans the columns its width needs. A build stands on the runtimes its consumers declare and on every required peer its parts carry, which the installer adds whether or not a manifest names it; an optional peer no consumer declares stays off the model.

One scale. The family keeps sheet 2's mould exactly — 1 stud per 150 sloc rounded up the brick shapes, a course per 3 authored files. A runtime is baseplates at sheet 2's baseplate footprint, 8×6 = 48 studs, one stud per 150 sloc of one shipped build — the files reachable from its exports entries, the development condition where one is shipped (@lit-labs/ssr-client) — laid a plate at a time and stacked when it overflows, the remainder on top at its own size, three plates to a brick course: effect is 19 plates · 18 of 8×6 + a 4×4, eslint is 9 plates · 8 of 8×6 + a 6×6 and hono is 2 plates · 1 of 8×6 + a 4×4. Under 48 studs a runtime is one plate at its own size, the nearest rectangle: @lit-labs/ssr 3×3, @lit-labs/ssr-client 1×2, @uirouter/core 6×6, lit 1×1, mobx 6×6 and signal-polyfill 2×2. A package's dependencies are a number in the schedule rather than area on the plate: @lit-labs/ssr builds 1,083 lines over a closure of 28,345, @lit-labs/ssr-client builds 282 lines over a closure of 4,564 and lit builds 39 lines over a closure of 4,282. Every model on lit stands on a 1×1: nine of the ten builds, ten of eleven with the atlas app, and the runtime they run on is in its closure.
