GENERAL NOTES

Method — one ruler, two deploys. Every block on this plate is massed by SIDE() and HT() imported from sheet9.mjs — footprint side 10 · √files, floored at 18; height one pixel per 4,300 gzipped bytes, floored at 8 — and every file is weighed the way sheet 9 weighs it: raw bytes and gzip level 9, the honest wire measure, since the CDN serves compressed. The two halves stand on different grounds and say so. The docs deploy is data/census-shipped.json, origin/main @ bd10638a, the cabinet sheet 9 reads, summed here into sheet 9's three roles. The atlas is not on main, so it cannot be in that cabinet: it is data/survey-shipped.json, written by generator/survey-shipped.mjs from a git archive of its own branch — www/atlas @ 0ade0a3a, committed 2026-10-12 — in which the app is built by its own npm ci and npm run build, the vite build and the prerender, and app/dist is measured file by file: 252 files, 17.5 MB on disk, 7.2 MB on the wire. That is what Cloudflare Pages serves at atlas.lit-ui-router.dev. A reachability walk from every page sorts the files into districts, and a path the app computes at runtime — a fragment by the manifest's file, the manifest by the router's base, the visualizer's icons by its stylesheet — is seeded rather than reported as a false orphan.

What the drawing says. The atlas deploy is 1.5× the docs deploy it draws, on the wire: 7.2 MB gzipped against 4.8 MB, in 252 files against 898. The tallest district is card pictures, 3559 KB in 58 files — 2.0× the docs deploy's tallest, html pages at 1776 KB. The pictures are the whole story of the weight: a card picture is a webp, already compressed, so card pictures keep 99% of their raw bytes through gzip; the routed pages keep 24% — the lettering and markup of a deploy shrink on the wire and its pictures do not. The plates themselves ship in triplicate: every plate ships three times — inside its routed page, as a bare plate and as a fragment: 1101 KB + 664 KB + 606 KB — the routed page carries its plate server-rendered, the bare plate stands for a reader who opts out of the router, and the fragment feeds the client's own navigation. Below those, the 3D lane's viewer and its decoders, 854 KB, outweigh the routed app itself, 177 KB, 4.8 to 1, and no ghost district stands here: every file in the deploy is reached from a page, by reference or by a seeded path. Every claim in this paragraph is computed from the two plates and changes its wording when the plates change it.

Why aggregates, and why the atlas is not. The docs deploy stands as three summed blocks, one per role sheet 9 outlines, because the question is how much atlas there is on the wire, and sheet 9 already draws the docs deploy district by district. Summing is honest on this ruler for plan area — area ∝ files, so one block of a role's sum covers the same ground its districts do — and approximate for height, which reads a role's total bytes as one tower. The atlas keeps its ten districts because it has no other drawing that shows its parts. Sheet 9's role membership is read from its exported ROLES, and a district sheet 9 draws that no role claims is a build error there, so the two plates cannot disagree.

What the pictures are for. The 58 card pictures are the cover's cards: one per plate in each theme, 300×400 CSS pixels drawn at 2×, so a card holds up on a retina display and the cyanotype theme has a picture of its own rather than an inverted one. They are 48% of the wire and arrive lazily, as the gallery scrolls; the first page a reader lands on is 275 KB at most, the heaviest routed page, and a plate's own page carries nothing of the pictures at all. The weight is real and the drawing shows it; whether it is the right weight is a question for the cover, not for this ruler.
