GENERAL NOTES

This plate is the legibility companion to sheet 2. The exploded assembly (sheet 2, THE BRICK ASSEMBLY) shows the whole stack and where every brick falls; this plate isolates the couplings and draws each one at reading size. The arrangement answers to reading rather than to assembly — core central, companions at its right, the server in a request lane below the no-DOM line — and the packages are shallow solids, so the recovered space goes entirely to the joints.

Every joint is drawn disengaged. A seated plug hides both mating faces, so nothing here is seated: each stud on the wall stops 18px short of its plug’s mouth, the dashed centreline is the engagement, and the call that makes the coupling — stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder), urlService.listen()/.sync(), router.plugin(servicesPlugin), transitionService.onSuccess({}, update), globals.current/.params — is lettered on the connection itself, not in a schedule at the edge of the sheet.

One connection point is a seat, not a stud, and it is drawn as a keyed recess. A router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin does not add to the wall — it swaps: its keyed red plug approaches the LOCATION SEAT while a dashed ghost plug, core’s own pushStateLocation, leaves it. The rail above behaves the opposite way: router.plugin() is one method with any number of seats, which is where visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx would queue (sheet 4).

The seat under the flagship gets its own axis. lit-ui-router-mobx takes it: its upward socket waits under a stud on lit-ui-router’s underside — seekRouter(host), the bubbling ui-router-context event it falls back to when no context-request provider answers — and it holds no hook of its own: it takes RouterStore.for() from ui-router-mobx ^0.2.0 (block 10) through a stud on that block's face, and reacts to the store, never a write.

Eight joints never touch the wall, and one of them spans the sheet. They come from four companions, and four of those reach two blocks. lit-ui-router-mobx reaches two: the seat under block 1, ^1.16.0, and a stud on block 10's face, ^0.2.0. The other three leave studs on block 1's right face as nested L's, the highest arm turning down farthest out, so no arm crosses another. lit-ui-router-ssr (block 6, 18 files, 1,485 sloc) takes the highest: it peers lit-ui-router ^1.19.0 and ui-router-server ^0.7.0, so its arm runs down the right edge of the plate, crosses the no-DOM line, and meets a second stud on block 4's face below it. Nothing else here crosses; a package that prerenders Lit for a server router is exactly the thing that would. lit-ui-router-effect (block 5, 4 files, 285 sloc) takes the middle stud, ^1.16.0, and plugs a stud on top of ui-router-effect ^0.2.0, whose routeRef() and snapshotRoute() it re-exports and binds to a Lit host. lit-ui-router-signals (block 7, 4 files, 170 sloc) takes the lowest, ^1.16.0, and plugs a stud on top of ui-router-signals ^0.2.0, whose RouterSignals.for() and watchSelection() it binds to a Lit host. Each binding stands over the block it plugs and holds no hook of its own.

Three blocks tap the wall and plug no companion. ui-router-effect (block 8, 30 sloc), ui-router-signals (block 9, 104 sloc) and ui-router-mobx (block 10, 117 sloc) peer @uirouter/core and nothing in this repo: each holds one onSuccess hook on stud C and reads globals on stud E, into an Effect SubscriptionRef, a Signal.State or MobX observables. Block 10 holds the C and E plugs, and a dashed tap runs from blocks 8 and 9 back to its E plug, because those are the studs block 10 plugs: one stud, a hook per consumer. Each carries a stud for its Lit binding — blocks 8 and 9 on the top face, block 10 on its right face, beside block 3 — and the binding plugs it. Sheet 2 seats all three on the plate; this plan seats nothing, so it draws their joints.

The missing coupling is drawn only to be crossed out. ui-router-server takes no stud: @uirouter/core is an optional peer (peerDependenciesMeta), its default 'matcher' tier is dependency-free, and its 'simulate' tier lazily imports a wall of its own. Blocks are sized roughly by mass — front-face area ≈ 35 px² per sloc, from census-bricks.json counted at origin/main @ 3f55e419, which puts lit-ui-router at 3,262 sloc — with the two smallest companions (both 1×1) clamped up to a legible minimum, because at true scale they would be postage stamps, and their smallness is already sheet 2’s finding.
