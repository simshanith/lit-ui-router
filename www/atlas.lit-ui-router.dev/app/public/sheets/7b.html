The rule is the plate's stated method, mechanised: sort the distinct readings, measure the joints between them, and cut at the widest. The top step is reserved for a true outlier and opens only when the topmost joint is also the widest in the distribution; otherwise R4 is pinned above the highest reading and stands EMPTY. At this cabinet it opens: the topmost joint, 56d to 75d, is the widest in the distribution. A step label therefore means nothing across cabinets — the cuts move with the distribution, which is why they are printed in the telemetry box rather than remembered. The channels disagree, which is the point. A single wreck-to-splendor axis would have to average these stories away. lit-ui-router is the oldest masonry in the city and its hottest steam and fully lamped — old and running. And @tools/happy-dom keeps plate 7A's oddest light: a plant whose spec annex is a canary pointed upstream, lighting only the workaround it guards (42.9% lit).

Every channel by import, and the ladder cuts itself. Placements, districts and gate tiers are imported from sheet 7's own placement table, and the masses from www/atlas.lit-ui-router.dev/data/census-city.json, so the two sheets cannot drift building for building. Rust, steam, lamps and pipes are each looked up by member directory in their own filed plate, and a member this sheet draws that a plate does not carry is a build error rather than a stale number. Rust was the last channel drawn by hand — an editorial step per member, keyed by badge — and it is not any more: the step is the ladder above applied to the weather plate's own medIdle, so 7B and sheet 13 cannot disagree about how long a member has stood idle, and a member with no dated source (@tools/wintercg-globals) carries no step rather than a guessed zero. The steam total — 365 member-touches from 546 window commits — double-counts commits touching several members, as any per-member count must, so the window commit count is printed beside it.
