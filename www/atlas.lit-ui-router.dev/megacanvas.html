GENERAL NOTES

Why redraw a 2D loop in 3D: the cycle itself is flat, but the third axis is free to carry the relations flat arrows kept fumbling. Containment: the child <ui-view> literally stands on its parent's roof — nesting is stacking, not a box-in-a-box. Instrumentation: TransitionController is a skybridge that taps the hall without standing on the route.

The document breaks the metaphor on purpose. Every other station is a building; the document is drawn the way Firefox's old Tilt inspector drew it — a browser window whose DOM rises as stacked plates by depth, window → body → app shell → <ui-view> content → the <a> element on top. It is the one structure on this sheet that really is layered, so it alone earns the literal treatment.

Service discovery is still DOM events. A nested view finds its parent with a composed context CustomEvent; every uiSref announces its targetState upward. The document tree is the dependency graph — which is exactly why the drawing can stand everything in one window.

The six doors are one seam. The bare entry registers custom elements as a side effect; ./pure is the same API with registration torn off. sideEffects in the manifest names exactly the four register files.

the BRICK ASSEMBLY

ALTITUDE 2 — one baseplate, four bricks, 35 authored files · an exploded LEGO assembly with every coupling named to the API call that makes it, brick and stud faces drawn opaque so nothing reads through a mass in front of it · source counted at origin/main @ 4223ffc7 (2026-09-14)

GENERAL NOTES

Why bricks. The mechanism these packages share is a standardised coupling: each companion attaches to @uirouter/core through a published extension point, none of them attaches to another, and any one can be left in the box without disturbing the rest. That is a stud, and a stud is worth drawing. So this is an exploded isometric — the LEGO instruction manual's own idiom — with a numbered part per package, a drop line onto the exact stud it takes, and a parts callout. Nothing is drawn seated, because a seated assembly hides the undersides, and the undersides are the argument.

The plate is core, not this package. Sheet 4's finding decides it: every limb in the family declares @uirouter/core as a peer and touches nothing else. lit-ui-router is therefore brick 1, not the ground — and the drawing is honest about the one place the metaphor strains: class UIRouterLit extends UIRouter is moulded onto the plate, not snapped to it. What the drawing then shows is that the graft is thin anyway. Everything Lit-specific arrives through two published seams — this.plugin(servicesPlugin) and this.stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder) — plus urlService.listen()/sync() to start the thing. Three calls, and one internal seam — viewService._pluginapi._viewConfigFactory('lit', …) — that core does not publish. That is the whole renderer coupling.

One stud is a seat, not a socket. router.plugin() is the back rail: a single method that anything may queue on, which is why the spare-parts box is drawn at all — @uirouter/visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx all register through it and none of them knows this repo exists. One seat on that rail is ringed red, because it behaves differently: a router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin is a swap for core's pushStateLocation, never an addition. That package peers @uirouter/core and nothing else — it has never heard of Lit, and would work identically under the React or Angular adapters.

Brick 3 is the only brick-to-brick coupling in the set, and it is a small one. lit-ui-router-mobx seats on brick 1 through UIRouterLitElement.seekRouter(host) — a bubbling ui-router-context event, published precisely as the dependency-injection primitive for external reactivity systems — and then reaches the plate directly: one transitionService.onSuccess({}, update) hook, memoised one-per-router by RouterStore.for(), whose update() reads globals.current, globals.params and the last successful transition into MobX observables. It observes; it never writes router state. 176 lines, one stud on the brick above and two on the plate below.

The fourth brick has no stud here at all, and that is the design. ui-router-server declares @uirouter/core as an optional peer (peerDependenciesMeta); its default 'matcher' tier is dependency-free pattern matching and never loads core, and its 'simulate' tier reaches a plate of its own behind a lazy import() — new UIRouter() with servicesPlugin and memoryLocationPlugin, built fresh per resolution because core mutates registrations. Drawing it over a dashed second plate is the only truthful placement: it is the same mould, a different assembly, and the tie back to the client plate is crossed out.

Massing, quantized. Continuous mass would have made these bricks unbuildable shapes, so the census is rounded to LEGO: plan is one stud per 150 sloc rounded up to the next standard shape (1×1, 1×2, 2×2, 2×3, 2×4), height is one course per three authored files. The result is legible and it is a finding — the two bricks that carry a renderer and a server are 2×4s; the two that plug the router into something are a 1×1 and a 1×2 tile, 105 and 176 lines. A companion that needed to be a 2×4 would be lit-ui-router's problem to absorb, not a package. The baseplate is deliberately not massed: 80 files and 5,272 lines of core is ground, and ground has no height.

Seven published packages; four bricks in this assembly. Two more bricks are moulded and not seated here. lit-ui-router-effect 0.1.0-rc.0 (5 files, 211 sloc) takes the coupling brick 3 takes — @uirouter/core ^6.0.8 as a peer and lit-ui-router ^1.7.0 above it — over a second reactivity layer. lit-ui-router-ssr 0.1.0-rc.0 (2 files, 253 sloc) peers lit-ui-router ^1.15.0 and ui-router-server ^0.2.0, which makes it the one part in the family that would take a drop line onto each of this sheet's two plates. Both are scheduled, as rows 5 and 6, rather than crowded into a drawing whose four bricks are lettered and leadered on every side; seating them is a recomposition, not an addition. The seventh package, eslint-plugin-lit-ui-router 1.1.0 (12 files, 1,317 sloc), is not a brick at all: it takes no stud on any router plate, because it couples to ESLint and oxlint, not to @uirouter/core. Every number on this sheet is read from www/atlas.lit-ui-router.dev/data/census-bricks.json — counted at origin/main @ 4223ffc7 (2026-09-14).

ALTITUDE 6 — routing in general · the altitude where prose outranks pictures

GENERAL NOTES

The definition that survives every stratum: a router is an address space, a matching function, and transition semantics. BGP matches prefixes and forwards with no memory; an HTTP framework matches path patterns into a stateless response; an edge ruleset rewrites and proxies; a SPA router matches a URL into a view tree through a cancellable lifecycle; a state router matches into a named hierarchy through guarded, resolving transitions.

What changes with ascent is the transition. At the bedrock a transition is a forwarding decision that forgets itself immediately. Each stratum upward, transitions acquire more: statefulness, interceptability, cancellation, resolved data, meaning. By the surface a transition is a first-class object with a lifecycle — which is precisely the layer sheet 1 drew.
