GENERAL NOTES

Why bricks. The mechanism these packages share is a standardised coupling: each companion attaches through a published extension point — on @uirouter/core, or on a brick below it that publishes one — and any brick nothing stands on can be left in the box without disturbing the rest. That is a stud, and a stud is worth drawing. So this is an exploded isometric — the LEGO instruction manual's own idiom — with a numbered part per package, a drop line onto the exact stud it takes, and a parts callout. The exploded view seats nothing, because a seated assembly hides the undersides, and the undersides are the argument; the finished model under it is drawn seated for the other half of the reading — the shape the parts make — in three steps at the drawing's own corner and then whole from two more, on a landscape of two levels: browser ground under the client plate, and a server shelf under the headless one.

The plate is core, not this package. Sheet 4's finding decides it: every limb in the family declares @uirouter/core as a peer. lit-ui-router is therefore brick 1, not the ground — and the drawing is honest about the one place the metaphor strains: class UIRouterLit extends UIRouter is moulded onto the plate, not snapped to it. What the drawing then shows is that the graft is thin anyway. Everything Lit-specific arrives through two published seams — this.plugin(servicesPlugin) and this.stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder) — plus urlService.listen()/sync() to start the thing. Three calls, and one internal seam — viewService._pluginapi._viewConfigFactory('lit', …) — that core does not publish. That is the whole renderer coupling.

One stud is a seat, not a socket. router.plugin() is the back rail: a single method that anything may queue on, which is why the spare-parts box is drawn at all — @uirouter/visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx all register through it and none of them knows this repo exists. One seat on that rail is ringed red, because it behaves differently: a router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin is a swap for core's pushStateLocation, never an addition. That package peers @uirouter/core and nothing else — it has never heard of Lit, and would work identically under the React or Angular adapters.

Brick 1 publishes two studs of its own, and three bricks take them. lit-ui-router-mobx and lit-ui-router-effect both ask first over stud G, requestRouter(host, { subscribe: true }), so a placeholder router's upgrade reaches them, and seat through UIRouterLitElement.seekRouter(host) — stud F, a bubbling ui-router-context event, published precisely as the dependency-injection primitive for external reactivity systems — when no provider answers; then they reach the plate directly, each with one transitionService.onSuccess({}, …) hook per router whose handler reads globals.current, globals.params and the last successful transition: into MobX observables through RouterStore.attach(), into one Effect SubscriptionRef through routeRef(). Both observe; neither writes router state. 204 and 288 lines, two studs on the brick below and two on the plate. lit-ui-router-ssr takes stud G alone: lit-ui-router/context, the community context-request protocol spoken without @lit/context. prerender() provides the router on the render root with provideRouter() and scopes each render with withRouterSync(), its UiViewRenderer reads that scope with getScopedRouter(), and on the client the served view it defines — withServedRender(UiView), brick 1's own view class extended — wakes on requestContext(adoptUiViewContext), which hydrateRoot() answers with provideContext().

The fourth brick has no stud on the client plate, and that is the design. ui-router-server declares @uirouter/core as an optional peer (peerDependenciesMeta); its default 'matcher' tier is dependency-free pattern matching and never loads core, and its 'simulate' tier reaches a plate of its own behind a lazy import() — new UIRouter() with servicesPlugin and memoryLocationPlugin, built fresh per resolution because core mutates registrations. Drawing it over a dashed second plate is the only truthful placement: it is the same mould, a different assembly, and the tie back to the client plate is crossed out. Seated, that plate stands on a shelf of its own: server ground, a request runtime with no DOM (node >=22.12; Connect, Vite, fetch and Hono adapters), drawn five courses above the browser ground because brick 6 must meet brick 4's cap at the height of brick 1's. The plate on it is dashed — an optional peer, reached by lazy import — and the ground is not. It does carry a stud of its own, H — createServerRouter({ mounts }), whose resolve(path) returns a verdict.

Brick 6 is the bridge. lit-ui-router-ssr peers lit-ui-router ^1.16.1 and ui-router-server ^0.2.0, and it is the one part in the family that takes a drop line onto each of this sheet's two assemblies: G on brick 1, H on brick 4. prerender() compiles the mount table with createServerRouter(), asks it for a verdict per path, and turns shell verdicts into pages, redirects into host rules and the otherwise projection into the 404 document. It never reaches the second plate's studs itself — that plate is ui-router-server's to load or not. Its other two peers, @lit-labs/ssr ^4.0.0 and @lit-labs/ssr-client ^1.1.0, are a render table, not a router plate, so they take no stud here.

Massing, quantized. Continuous mass would have made these bricks unbuildable shapes, so the census is rounded to LEGO: plan is one stud per 150 sloc rounded up to the next standard shape (1×1, 1×2, 2×2, 2×3, 2×4), height is one course per three authored files. The result is legible and it is a finding — the three bricks that carry a renderer, a server and the prerender bridge between them are all 2×4s; the three that plug the router into something are a 1×1 and two 1×2s tiles, 141, 204 and 288 lines. A plug that needed to be a 2×4 would be lit-ui-router's problem to absorb, not a package. The baseplate is deliberately not massed: 80 files and 5,272 lines of core is ground, and ground has no height.

Seven published packages; six bricks in this assembly. Every runtime companion is drawn. the ROUTING STRATA

ALTITUDE 6 — routing in general · the altitude where prose outranks pictures

GENERAL NOTES

The definition that survives every stratum: a router is an address space, a matching function, and transition semantics. BGP matches prefixes and forwards with no memory; an HTTP framework matches path patterns into a stateless response; an edge ruleset rewrites and proxies; a SPA router matches a URL into a view tree through a cancellable lifecycle; a state router matches into a named hierarchy through guarded, resolving transitions.

What changes with ascent is the transition. At the bedrock a transition is a forwarding decision that forgets itself immediately. Each stratum upward, transitions acquire more: statefulness, interceptability, cancellation, resolved data, meaning. By the surface a transition is a first-class object with a lifecycle — which is precisely the layer sheet 1 drew.

Why this sheet is mostly writing: no mechanism spans these strata — an IP hop and a ui-view activation share a definition, not a wire. Drawing more than a core sample here would be decoration, and the reference's own rule applies: the drawing earns exactly as much ink as the mechanism deserves.
