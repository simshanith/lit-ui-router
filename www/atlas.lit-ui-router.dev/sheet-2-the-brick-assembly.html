GENERAL NOTES

Why bricks. The mechanism these packages share is a standardised coupling: each companion attaches through a published extension point — on @uirouter/core, or on a brick below it that publishes one — and any brick nothing stands on can be left in the box without disturbing the rest. That is a stud, and a stud is worth drawing. So this is an exploded isometric — the LEGO instruction manual's own idiom — with a numbered part per package, a drop line onto the exact stud it takes, and a parts callout. Nothing is drawn seated, because a seated assembly hides the undersides, and the undersides are the argument.

The plate is core, not this package. Sheet 4's finding decides it: every limb in the family declares @uirouter/core as a peer. lit-ui-router is therefore brick 1, not the ground — and the drawing is honest about the one place the metaphor strains: class UIRouterLit extends UIRouter is moulded onto the plate, not snapped to it. What the drawing then shows is that the graft is thin anyway. Everything Lit-specific arrives through two published seams — this.plugin(servicesPlugin) and this.stateRegistry.decorator('views', litViewsBuilder) — plus urlService.listen()/sync() to start the thing. Three calls, and one internal seam — viewService._pluginapi._viewConfigFactory('lit', …) — that core does not publish. That is the whole renderer coupling.

One stud is a seat, not a socket. router.plugin() is the back rail: a single method that anything may queue on, which is why the spare-parts box is drawn at all — @uirouter/visualizer, sticky-states, dsr and rx all register through it and none of them knows this repo exists. One seat on that rail is ringed red, because it behaves differently: a router holds exactly one location plugin, so ui-router-navigation-location-plugin is a swap for core's pushStateLocation, never an addition. That package peers @uirouter/core and nothing else — it has never heard of Lit, and would work identically under the React or Angular adapters.

Brick 1 publishes two studs of its own, and three bricks take them. lit-ui-router-mobx and lit-ui-router-effect both seat through UIRouterLitElement.seekRouter(host) — stud F, a bubbling ui-router-context event, published precisely as the dependency-injection primitive for external reactivity systems — and then reach the plate directly, each with one transitionService.onSuccess({}, …) hook per router whose handler reads globals.current, globals.params and the last successful transition: into MobX observables through RouterStore.attach(), into one Effect SubscriptionRef through routeRef(). Both observe; neither writes router state. 176 and 211 lines, one stud on the brick below and two on the plate. lit-ui-router-ssr takes the other, stud G: lit-ui-router/context, the community context-request protocol spoken without @lit/context. prerender() provides the router on the render root with provideRouter() and scopes each render with withRouterSync(), its UiViewRenderer reads that scope with getScopedRouter(), and on the client the served view it defines — withServedRender(UiView), brick 1's own view class extended — wakes on requestContext(adoptUiViewContext), which hydrateRoot() answers with provideContext().

The fourth brick has no stud on the client plate, and that is the design. ui-router-server declares @uirouter/core as an optional peer (peerDependenciesMeta); its default 'matcher' tier is dependency-free pattern matching and never loads core, and its 'simulate' tier reaches a plate of its own behind a lazy import() — new UIRouter() with servicesPlugin and memoryLocationPlugin, built fresh per resolution because core mutates registrations. Drawing it over a dashed second plate is the only truthful placement: it is the same mould, a different assembly, and the tie back to the client plate is crossed out. It does carry a stud of its own, H — createServerRouter({ mounts }), whose resolve(path) returns a verdict.

Brick 6 is the bridge. lit-ui-router-ssr peers lit-ui-router ^1.16.0-rc.1 and ui-router-server ^0.2.0, and it is the one part in the family that takes a drop line onto each of this sheet's two assemblies: G on brick 1, H on brick 4. prerender() compiles the mount table with createServerRouter(), asks it for a verdict per path, and turns shell verdicts into pages, redirects into host rules and the otherwise projection into the 404 document. It never reaches the second plate's studs itself — that plate is ui-router-server's to load or not. Its other two peers, @lit-labs/ssr ^4.0.0 and @lit-labs/ssr-client ^1.1.0, are a render table, not a router plate, so they take no stud here.

Massing, quantized. Continuous mass would have made these bricks unbuildable shapes, so the census is rounded to LEGO: plan is one stud per 150 sloc rounded up to the next standard shape (1×1, 1×2, 2×2, 2×3, 2×4), height is one course per three authored files. The result is legible and it is a finding — the two bricks that carry a renderer and a server are 2×4s, and the prerender bridge between them a 2×3; the three that plug the router into something are a 1×1 and two 1×2s tiles, 141, 176 and 211 lines. A companion that needed to be a 2×4 would be lit-ui-router's problem to absorb, not a package. The baseplate is deliberately not massed: 80 files and 5,272 lines of core is ground, and ground has no height.

Seven published packages; six bricks in this assembly. Every runtime companion is drawn. The seventh package, eslint-plugin-lit-ui-router 1.1.0 (12 files, 1,317 sloc), is not a brick at all: it takes no stud on any router plate, because it couples to ESLint and oxlint, not to @uirouter/core.
